# ASD-STE100 Writing Rules — Working Summary

A condensed, independently written summary of the ASD-STE100 writing rules. It is not the specification and does not replace it. The specification is free from asd-ste100.org.

**Current edition: Issue 9, published 15 January 2025.** Issue 10 is scheduled for January 2028. Issue 9 carries 53 writing rules; this summary covers the ones that matter most in practice, grouped into 8 categories, so it is deliberately shorter than the source. Check anything load-bearing against the current issue.

Rule IDs below (W-1, V-2, …) are internal to this skill so that reports can reference them stably. They are not the specification's own numbering, and should not be quoted as such.

## 1. Words (W)

- **W-1** Use only approved words from the STE dictionary, in their approved meaning and approved part of speech. Example: "close" is approved as a verb ("Close the door"), not as an adjective ("do not go close to").
- **W-2** Technical Names (TN) are allowed: nouns/noun phrases for parts, tools, materials, systems, locations, circuits, documents (e.g. "main gear door", "grease", "circuit breaker panel").
- **W-3** Technical Verbs (TV) are allowed: verbs for manufacturing, maintenance, and operational actions (e.g. "drill", "ream", "torque", "calibrate").
- **W-4** One word, one meaning. Do not use the same word with different meanings in one document.
- **W-5** Do not use vague or "elegant variation" synonyms. Use the same approved word for the same thing every time.
- **W-6** Do not use "etc.", "e.g.", "i.e.", "and/or", or slashes as words. Write "for example", list the items, or restructure.

## 2. Noun clusters (N)

- **N-1** Maximum 3 nouns in a cluster. Break longer clusters with prepositions: "fuel tank access door" is at the limit; "fuel tank access door seal replacement" must be restructured.
- **N-2** Hyphenate or explain a cluster the first time if its structure is not obvious.
- **N-3** Clusters that are official Technical Names may stand as-is.

## 3. Verbs (V)

- **V-1** Use only simple verb forms: infinitive, imperative, simple present, simple past, simple future, past participle (only as adjective or with "to be" for a state).
- **V-2** Do not use -ing forms (gerunds, present participles): "Removing the pump…" → "When you remove the pump…". Exception: -ing words that are part of a Technical Name ("landing gear", "warning light").
- **V-3** Use active voice. Passive is a violation in procedural text and only tolerated in descriptive text when the doer is unknown or unimportant — prefer active there too.
- **V-4** Do not use "shall" or "should". Requirements: "must". Permissions/ability: "can". Future: "will".
- **V-5** Do not use "may" or "might". Use "can" (ability/possibility) or "it is possible".
- **V-6** Do not use complex tenses (present perfect, past perfect, continuous): "has been removed" → "was removed" or "is removed".
- **V-7** Do not omit the verb or use a noun where the dictionary approves the verb: "Do the adjustment of…" is wrong if "adjust" is the approved verb — write "Adjust…". (Reverse also applies: if only the noun is approved, use "do a/the <noun>".)

## 4. Sentences (S)

- **S-1** Procedural sentences: maximum 20 words.
- **S-2** Descriptive sentences: maximum 25 words.
- **S-3** One instruction per sentence. More than one action is allowed only when actions are simultaneous ("Hold the switch and turn the knob").
- **S-4** Do not omit words to shorten sentences (no telegraphic style). Keep articles ("the", "a") and demonstratives: "Remove bolt" → "Remove the bolt".
- **S-5** Use a vertical (numbered) list when a sentence would contain a series of items or steps.
- **S-6** Start a safety-relevant condition with the condition: "If the pressure is more than 100 psi, open the valve."
- **S-7** Do not use vague subjects "it is", "there is/there are" when a direct subject is possible: "There are three bolts that hold the panel" → "Three bolts hold the panel."

## 5. Procedures (P)

- **P-1** Write instructions in the imperative: "Disconnect the connector."
- **P-2** Write instructions in the correct sequence of execution.
- **P-3** Do not bury an instruction inside a description. Separate them.
- **P-4** Give necessary preconditions before the action, not after: "Before you open the valve, put on gloves." not "Put on gloves after opening…" reversed.

## 6. Descriptive text (D)

- **D-1** Maximum 6 sentences per paragraph.
- **D-2** One topic per paragraph; the first sentence is the topic sentence.
- **D-3** Vary sentence length; do not chain many maximum-length sentences.
- **D-4** Use connecting words from the approved dictionary ("thus", "then", "but") to show relationships between sentences.

## 7. Warnings, cautions, notes (C)

- **C-1** WARNING = risk of injury or death. CAUTION = risk of equipment damage. NOTE = information only. Do not mix these levels.
- **C-2** Warnings and cautions come **before** the step they apply to. Notes come after the related text.
- **C-3** Start a warning/caution with a simple and clear command, then give the reason: "DO NOT TOUCH THE VALVE. THE VALVE IS HOT."
- **C-4** Do not put procedural steps inside a note.

## 8. Punctuation and layout (X)

- **X-1** Use a colon to introduce a vertical list; use numbers for sequential steps, dashes/letters for non-sequential items.
- **X-2** Use hyphens in noun clusters to show what modifies what ("high-pressure valve").
- **X-3** Use parentheses only for references, abbreviations at first use, and letters/numbers of list items — not for asides that belong in the sentence.
- **X-4** Write abbreviations out at first use with the abbreviation in parentheses; use approved abbreviations after that.

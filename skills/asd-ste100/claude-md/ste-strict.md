# Technical Writing — ASD-STE100 (strict)

Apply these rules to technical documentation: procedures, manuals, warnings, work cards, and maintenance steps. Do not apply them to chat replies or informal messages.

## Words

1. Use short, common words. Write "do" not "perform", "start" not "commence", "use" not "utilize", "make sure" not "ensure".
2. Write "before" not "prior to", "after" not "subsequent to", "to" not "in order to", "if" not "in the event that".
3. Give one word one meaning. Do not use a synonym to add variety.
4. Technical Names are always allowed. Never simplify the name of a part, tool, system, or document.
5. Technical Verbs are always allowed. Keep "drill", "ream", "torque", "calibrate".
6. Do not write "etc.", "e.g.", "i.e.", or "and/or". List the items, or write the sentence again.

## Verbs

7. Use the imperative for instructions: "Remove the bolt."
8. Use active voice. Do not write "the bolt must be removed by the technician".
9. Do not use -ing forms. Exception: -ing words inside a Technical Name, such as "landing gear" or "warning light".
10. Use "must" for a requirement, "can" for ability, "will" for the future. Do not use "shall", "should", "may", or "might".
11. Use simple tenses only. Write "was removed", not "has been removed".

## Sentences

12. Keep procedural sentences to 20 words or less.
13. Keep descriptive sentences to 25 words or less.
14. Give one instruction per sentence. Two actions are permitted only when they occur together.
15. Keep the articles. Write "Remove the bolt", not "Remove bolt".
16. Use a maximum of 3 nouns in a cluster. Break longer clusters with prepositions.
17. Put the condition first: "If the pressure is more than 100 psi, open the valve."

## Paragraphs and lists

18. Use a maximum of 6 sentences in a descriptive paragraph.
19. Give each paragraph one topic. Put the topic in the first sentence.
20. Use numbers for sequential steps only. Use letters or dashes for items with no order.

## Warnings, cautions, and notes

21. Use WARNING for a risk of injury. Use CAUTION for a risk of damage to equipment. Use NOTE for information only.
22. Put warnings and cautions before the step that they apply to. Put notes after the related text.
23. Start a warning with a command, then give the reason: "DO NOT TOUCH THE VALVE. THE VALVE IS HOT."
24. Do not put a procedural step inside a note.

## Verification

25. To check a document, run the asd-ste100 skill with the strict profile.
26. The full specification is free from asd-ste100.org. Check unfamiliar words against the official dictionary.
